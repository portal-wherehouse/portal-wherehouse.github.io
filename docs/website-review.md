# Website decisions — September 29, 2026

The website should answer the buyer's practical questions: Is this for my warehouse? What does the daily work look like? What does it cost? Will my equipment work? Who helps us get started?

## Evidence and its limits

- [TrustRadius, 2026 B2B Buying Disconnect](https://www.trustradius.com/blog/beyond-the-hype): transparent pricing remains buyers' most requested change, with 59% selecting it. The report also emphasizes peer validation and product experience. It surveys general B2B technology buyers, not just warehouse managers, and does not establish that a particular page layout causes purchases.
- [Nielsen Norman Group, B2B website usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-usability/): useful product detail and visible pricing support buyers doing their own research. This is older usability research; use its practical questions, not a claimed current conversion benchmark.

My application of that evidence: give someone enough information to decide without a sales call, let them inspect the actual program, and explain implementation/support plainly. A compact table can be more useful than another polished slogan. Warehouse-specific proof still needs actual pilot observation; no invented customer logos, testimonials, savings percentages or success claims have been added.

## Changes

- How it works explains receiving, put-away and dispatch with three short steps, then separates floor and management tasks.
- Why Wherehouse compares ordinary questions answered using notes/memory versus shared pallet records. It also says when a fuller WMS would fit better.
- Pricing keeps $29/month unchanged, compares using existing equipment with having equipment/setup arranged, and includes remote support. Hardware and on-site work are quoted before commitment; customers can use their own hardware and labels or obtain them from Wherehouse.
- Get help has a clear email contact and distinct setup/equipment/on-site help information.
- Printing guidance keeps the useful practical details but replaces the long low-cost-printer pitch with a short equipment choice and link to plans.
- Internal marketing-page headings are smaller and lighter, including mobile. No manufactured loading delay was added; existing page transitions remain and actual data loading has a status message.
- The public sample starts with management or employee dashboard choices. Six generic example pallets, two jobs and four locations replace the noisy fixture. Short inline notes explain screens without blocking interaction or starting a guided tour. Development fixtures still exist for comprehensive tests.

## What to prove next

Before publishing customer evidence, watch one real unload, put-away, search and dispatch. Record search time, unrecorded moves, label readability and questions asked during training. Ask whether the crew would keep using the product and what setup/support it needs. A simulation and emulator test establish software behavior; they do not establish customer demand or operational adoption.
